\section{The Checkpoint-Noise Proxy: Reproduced Here, Unverifiable There}
\label{sec:t1}

Signal \& Noise \citep{signalandnoise2025} proposes that the spread of a training run's final
checkpoints can stand in for seed retraining: compute the relative SD of the last $n$
checkpoints of a \emph{single} run, and read it as the relative SD one would get by re-running
with new seeds. We reproduce the home result, port the proxy to its advertised use case, test it
as a magnitude substitute, and cross-check it on independent 10-seed ground truth (tables and
proofs: Appendix~\ref{app:t1}).

\paragraph{Replication in the home setting.}
\label{sec:t1-home}
On the paper's own released runs (Table~\ref{tab:t1a}) we obtain $R^2 = 0.81$/$0.99$ (init,
log/raw) and $0.88$/$0.98$ (data-order), matching two of the three published values ($0.82$
seed noise, $0.86$ data-order noise, and $0.95$ checkpoint-step noise; the range quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:intro}); the calibration
ratio $y/x$ has median $0.98$. S\&N define noise as the \emph{raw} relative SD but
do not state the correlation's space; our log-space replication matches their values and our
raw-space replication exceeds them --- we report both
throughout. (On the bpb readout the home correlation is weaker: $R^2 = 0.58$--$0.61$
log-space; Table~\ref{tab:t1a}.) \textbf{The claim holds in its original setting, and is
quantitatively calibrated there}; everything that follows is about transfer.

\paragraph{Porting to the advertised use case: the magnitude gap and the ranking failure.}
\label{sec:t1-port}
The advertised use is small-scale work: many recipes, few seeds, public checkpoint grids;
Signal \& Noise itself applies the proxy exactly there, computing noise from the final five
checkpoints of the 60M--750M DataDecide models. We
port the proxy to the same asset (14 sizes $\times$ 25 recipes, $R$ across the 10 OLMES
tasks per cell; 350 cells; detail: Table~\ref{tab:t1b}). Two results carry the transfer.

\textbf{Magnitude.} Pooling all 3{,}497 cells
(size $\times$ recipe $\times$ task; the 350 above are macro-only --- $3{,}500$ grid points minus
three degenerate ones: 1B \texttt{arc\_challenge} on DCLM-Baseline 50\%/Dolma 50\% has zero
seed variance, and 4M \texttt{boolq}/\texttt{piqa} on two Dolma variants have zero checkpoint
spread), the
calibration ratio $y/x$ has median $1.47$, or $1.61$ after the log-space small-sample
correction (median-basis; the correction is exact only for iid checkpoint noise ---
read $1.5$--$1.6$ as the range; Appendix~\ref{app:t1-proof}): the proxy \emph{underestimates} true
seed noise by about $1.6\times$ (a family$\times$task$\times$seed bootstrap puts the
dependence-matched standard error of the pooled median at $0.03$--$0.06$; only $53.9\%$ of
cells fall
within a $2\times$ band --- and that share is already evidence, because a \emph{perfect}
proxy under the same sampling noise would land only $67.9\%$ of cells inside the band),
reaching
$2.2$--$2.4\times$ at 6M/60M/150M/300M. On perplexity the same proxy fails in the
\emph{opposite} direction ($1.1$--$7\times$ over; below): the bias direction
is metric-dependent. \textbf{Ranking.} We also stress-test a use S\&N never advertised (they rank
\emph{benchmarks} by noise): ranking \emph{recipes} by their noise. There
the proxy is no better than chance at every scale (per-scale median Kendall $\tau = 0.03$;
top-5 overlap $10$--$40\%$). The failure is the proxy's, not the task's: a \emph{perfect}
proxy ranked against the same 3-seed truth would reach median $\tau \approx 0.53$
($0.49$--$0.56$ across scales) under the same sampling noise (Appendix~\ref{app:t1}).

\textbf{The advertised correlation, in the background.} The observed correlation falls short
of the advertised level (per-size medians $0.53$--$0.77$), and at this seed budget the
conventional (Fisher-$z$) certificate of $\lambda \ge 0.9$ cannot attain its nominal level:
a sample-complexity bound
(Proposition~\ref{prop:t1-bound}, Appendix~\ref{app:t1-proof}) puts certification at
$k \ge 266$--$1{,}516$ tasks at $80\%$ power, or $n \ge 135$ seeds per arm at the denser
$m{=}30$ checkpoint budget (at the released $m \le 5$ no seed budget suffices at the
reference dispersion; Appendix~\ref{app:t1-proof}), and the Monte-Carlo calibration at the
panel's own operating point reads Type-I $0.069$ at a nominal $0.05$ with power $0.12$
(Appendix~\ref{app:t1-mc}).
This is a statement about what anyone could certify under the stated model with the
conventional test, not about this proxy failing its own
calibration; the proxy's own advertised $0.9$ was itself a point estimate, not a certified
bound. (The Table~\ref{tab:lambda-loo} porting intervals use plug-in attenuation, so an
interval above $0.9$ --- as at 90M --- is not a level-$\alpha$ certificate; caption there.)

\paragraph{A candidate account: the seed-semantics gap.}
\label{sec:t1-semantics}
The $1.6\times$ factor admits a second, simpler reading. The proxy was calibrated on
\emph{single-source} seed noise, while DataDecide's seeds bundle init and data order
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}). On S\&N's 1B arms, independent additivity prices bundled noise at
$\sqrt{\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}^2 + \hat\sigma_{\mathrm{order}}^2}/\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}
= 1.62\times$ the init-only level (median over 9 primary tasks; Table~\ref{tab:t2bundle}) ---
equal to the proxy's apparent underestimate ($1.61$; ratio $1.00$). Rescaling the proxy by the
semantics factor lifts the in-$2\times$-band cell share from $54\%$ to $61\%$ (perfect-proxy
null $68\%$): the semantics reading covers most but not all of the gap. The 10-seed PolyPythias
cross-check shares the semantics (its seeds likewise bundle init with a pre-shuffled data
order) and reads $1.8\times$ with the two divergent \textsc{blimp}-31m runs included,
$1.3\times$ without. The semantics reading is a
hypothesis compatible with the 1B evidence, and we flag it as such: it does not survive
transfer to 20M, where the init and order
main effects are undetectable at the crossed grid's power and the same proxy under-reads
by $2.4$--$4.0\times$ even at a ten-seed budget (point estimates; the intervals are wide
enough to contain the 1B factor) --- a single-point observation on a different
recipe and a re-implemented stack, directional rather than a scale law
(Appendix~\ref{app:crossed}) --- so the $1.62\times$ factor is a 1B statement, not a 20M one. \textbf{The honest claim: the proxy is calibrated to whatever its training seeds varied;
at 1B that is consistent with a single source plus a predictable ${\sim}1.6\times$ semantics
gap; across the public grid the gap is non-monotone in scale (Table~\ref{tab:t1b}), and our
single 20M control reads larger ($2.4$--$4.0\times$, directional).} Whether the gap is recipe- and task-stable
is the question Section~\ref{sec:t2} shows to be undecidable on public arms.

\paragraph{Continuous metrics and an independent cross-check.}
\label{sec:t1-ppl}
On DataDecide's 11-domain perplexity tables the raw proxy fails in the \emph{opposite}
direction: it \emph{overestimates} seed noise, by $1.1$--$7\times$ in per-size medians, from
trend contamination on coarse grids (only at the two largest scales does detrending bring both
the median within $10\%$ of one --- $1.03$--$1.08$ --- and a majority of cells inside the
$2\times$ band; Table~\ref{tab:t1c}); the bias
direction is metric- and grid-dependent, not a stable property one could correct for.
PolyPythias's 10-seed ground truth confirms the underestimate independently
(Appendix~\ref{app:t1-pp}): median $1.8\times$ over the 9 available cells ($1.3\times$
excluding two divergent \textsc{blimp} 31m runs; Appendix~\ref{app:atlas}), worst
$4.2$--$5.2\times$ on saturated tasks where
step noise collapses but seed noise does not.

\paragraph{Verdict and usage license.}
\label{sec:t1-license}
\textbf{Verdict: calibrated at home; in the field, compatible with its ceiling but
uncertifiable --- and reading a narrower noise than advertised.} The observed correlation
sits below the advertised $0.9$ but matches a \emph{perfect} proxy's read at this seed budget
(de-attenuated $\hat\lambda$ median $0.93$; $0.89$ with LOO porting, intervals below $0.9$ at
4M/6M/10M --- 8M excepted, $[0.60, 0.92]$; Tables~\ref{tab:t1b}~and~\ref{tab:lambda-loo}): the correlation claim
survives transfer, and the advertised level stays beyond the conventional certificate's reach
at 3 seeds
(median ceiling $0.66$--$0.82$; Corollary~\ref{cor:t1-n3}) --- a statement about what anyone
could certify, not about this proxy failing its own calibration. What fails is the \emph{magnitude} reading: on
bundled-seed assets the proxy reads low by a factor that matches the single-source
vs.\ bundled semantics gap (${\sim}1.6\times$; the semantics account is a hypothesis that
prices the gap at 1B and does not survive transfer to 20M; Section~\ref{sec:t1-semantics}),
and high on
coarse-grid perplexity ($1.1$--$7\times$; trend contamination). \textbf{License:} with a dense checkpoint grid, a
detrended
continuous metric, and explicit seed-semantics conversion, the proxy may be read
as a magnitude estimate within a $2\times$ band in the median; we observe the detrending repair
at the two largest scales only (750M, itself budget-truncated, and 1B) --- two points, not a
threshold; and even inside the license $37$--$46\%$ of cells fall outside the band, with the
repair non-monotone in scale (Appendix~\ref{app:t1-ppl}). In all other regimes (few-seed comparisons,
accuracy metrics at small scale, saturated tasks), seed noise must be measured.
