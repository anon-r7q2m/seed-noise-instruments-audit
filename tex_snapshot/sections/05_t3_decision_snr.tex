\section{Decision Replay: 80\% Correct at 150M, Accuracy-Readout Noise-Limited Below 90M}
\label{sec:t3}

DataDecide's headline number---rankings at a single 150M proxy scale pick the better 1B data
recipe with ${\sim}80\%$ pairwise accuracy---is the de facto warranty for small-scale recipe
selection (its realized argmax pick ranks 21st of 25 at 1B; details below). That number averages three \emph{per-seed} decision attempts; their Figure~1
(right) draws the envelope against compute. Their text names the confound our model resolves
(``it is difficult to distinguish evaluation variance from true crossovers''). We audit both
readings of the instrument on the
released tables: target $=$ each
recipe's 1B score; prediction $=$ proxy-scale final scores, under single seeds (their
protocol) and 3-seed means (our variant); decisions $=$ all $\binom{25}{2} = 300$ unordered
pairwise comparisons, per task and per scale (Appendix~\ref{app:t3}, Table~\ref{tab:t3}).

\paragraph{The claim reproduces exactly.}
\label{sec:t3-repro}
At 150M, macro-average pairwise accuracy is $80.2\%$ under their single-seed protocol: the
published ${\sim}80\%$ to the digit (Figure~\ref{fig:t3}, left, the final-checkpoint slice of
that envelope). With 3-seed means it reads $82.7\%$. What the envelope does not show: the
per-scale noise budget (Table~\ref{tab:snr}), the noise-band coverage, and the behaviour off
the recommended scale, where its own curve already dips below chance without comment (the 4M
inversion below).

\paragraph{What the residual 20\% is made of.}
\label{sec:t3-decomp}
We call a comparison \emph{seed-unstable} if the pairwise decision is not invariant within
the released seeds: $54\%$ of the 52 macro errors at 150M land on such pairs, a ${\sim}2\times$
enrichment over the base rate (pooled and task-level shares: Appendix~\ref{app:t3-hme}). The
count reports where errors land, not what caused them: the classifier judges
\emph{single-seed} decisions (DataDecide's own protocol; our variant averages three), and the
same three seeds define both instability and the target ranking.

A hierarchical measurement-error model makes both caveats quantitative (specification,
per-scale table, and a self-check reproducing the replay: Appendix~\ref{app:t3-hme},
Table~\ref{tab:hme}). $13.5\%$ [$8.3$, $57.0$] of the expected residual errors are
attributable to seed noise at all; the remainder is latent proxy--target discordance, which
no amount of seed averaging can remove --- though the interval does not exclude a majority
seed-noise share. (The three shares are strictly finer estimands: $54\%$ of observed errors land on
seed-unstable pairs, descriptive; $29\%$ the model's truly flippable share; $13.5\%$ the
share attributable to seed noise at all.) The realized argmax pick at 150M --- the usage DataDecide recommends --- is
\texttt{DCLM-Baseline (QC FW 3\%)}, ranking 21st of 25 at 1B ($-4.7$ macro points below the
best; the top-3 picks rank 21st/11th/3rd); a noiseless ranking would still forfeit $-3.9$; the split between seed noise and latent
discordance is not determined by these data. Read as
top-$k$ regret --- the practitioner's metric --- the realized top-1 pick forfeits $5.9$ macro
points at 4M and $4.7$ at 150M (descriptive replay; seed-resampled 95\% intervals
$[1.6, 7.2]$ and $[1.9, 5.3]$, both excluding zero; Appendix~\ref{app:t3-hme}).

\paragraph{The headline: the separability SNR.}
\label{sec:t3-snr}
Accuracy percentages bury the operational question: \emph{at a given proxy scale, how large is
the signal to rank, relative to the noise given?} Table~\ref{tab:snr}
computes, per scale, the separability SNR
(Section~\ref{sec:setup-notation}) and the share of the 300 recipe pairs whose mean
difference falls inside its pair-specific 95\% Welch noise band (a $z$ approximation
understates these shares; Appendix~\ref{app:t3}).

The gradient is stark, and it is not smooth (Table~\ref{tab:snr}, Figure~\ref{fig:snr}): the
deconvolved SNR, which removes the seed noise
counted inside the raw signal (Section~\ref{sec:setup-notation}), reads $0.30$ at
4M and $0.77$ at 20M, crosses $1$ at 60M ($1.23$), and jumps to $2.61$ at 90M
(paired-bootstrap 95\% CI on the ratio $[1.6, 3.0]$; $3.52$ at 1B); the
indistinguishable share falls from $94\%$ at 4M to $33\%$ at 1B. At 10M the deconvolved signal
truncates at zero (upper limit $0.43$); the observed $58.0\%$ there is what such an instrument
delivers, and per task the same 10M models still decide $64.0\%$ of pairs correctly --- macro
aggregation cancels opposing task-level signals (Appendix~\ref{app:t3}). \textbf{At every scale up to 60M the
deconvolved SNR stays $\le 1.3$ (upper 95\% limits below $1.7$), and ${\ge}83\%$ of pairs fall
inside their own noise band at 4M--20M.} On this asset and the accuracy readout, the envelope's lower
edge is crossed
between 60M and 90M --- the jump survives leave-one-recipe-out and appears in 6 of 10
task-level readouts (ranges: Appendix~\ref{app:t3-snr-ci}) --- well below the recommended
150M; the bpb readout reads higher
throughout (Appendix~\ref{app:metric-panel}), so the
noise-limited regime is the accuracy readout's, not the instrument family's. Nor is the SNR readout tautological
with accuracy: read per task, the per-task deconvolved SNR --- which never sees the 1B
target --- predicts the per-task pairwise accuracy: Spearman $+0.39$ at 4M rising monotonically to $+0.90$ at 150M and
$+0.92$ at 530M ($p<0.001$ at 150M and 530M; $n = 10$ tasks).

\paragraph{At the smallest scale, aggregation hurts.}
\label{sec:t3-inversion}
At 4M the macro decision accuracy is \emph{below the coin flip}, and seed averaging does not
rescue it:
$31.0\%$ correct with 3-seed means against $37.8\%$ with single seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:t3}; the gap's point estimate is $-6.8$pp, but the same gap computed on
unlinked recipe draws is centered at zero with a 95\% range of $\pm 7$pp --- the finding that
survives the checks below is the rank inversion, not the accuracy gap's sign). The 4M macro ranking
of the 25 recipes correlates with the 1B ranking at $r = -0.56$ (Pearson, $p = 0.004$), and
the correlation climbs monotonically to $+0.91$ at 530M (6M/8M/10M/14M:
$-0.19$/$+0.26$/$+0.28$/$+0.35$; the budget-truncated 750M row dips to $+0.87$). The inversion is a property of the
macro-average readout, not of any single task: per-task correlations are positive on 5 of the
10 tasks while the macro average flips negative --- an aggregation effect, as the tasks carry
different shares of the cross-recipe variance at the two scales. It survives the aggregation
rule (median, z-score, rank, min-max), a separate bpb proxy re-evaluation (S\&N's
construction, with a single-seed 1B target: $-0.35$), 9 of 10 leave-one-task-out drops
(worst case $+0.11$), and a family-cluster bootstrap (95\% CI $[-0.72, -0.08]$, $P(r{>}0)
\approx 0.01$); propagating the 3-seed
estimation noise at both ends (resampling the three seed values within each recipe,
jointly with the family clusters) widens the interval to $[-0.68, 0.08]$ --- the inversion is
stable to recipe resampling, and borderline once seed noise is priced in; and it is not an
artifact of the 1B target: the 4M macro ranking correlates at $-0.52$ to $-0.68$ with
\emph{every} larger scale's ranking (60M through 1B; Appendix~\ref{app:t3}). At 4M the \emph{observed} small-scale ordering is
negatively informative about the target
ordering (the latent sign is not excluded): averaging pays only when the latent proxy ranking is positively informative about
the target, and at SNR $\le 1$ that sign is unknown. A documented cause of such inversions --- repetition rates shifting with the
training budget \citep{zhou2026repetition} --- does not apply here: only 3 of the 25 recipe
pools repeat at 1B ($1.02$--$1.22$ epochs), and excluding them leaves $r = -0.53$. The
inversion is aggregation-level: restricted to the seven tasks with detectable above-chance 4M
signal under a target-free screen (the task's own chance rate, never the 1B target), it
attenuates but persists at $-0.33$ [$-0.54$, $0.16$]; the target-selected variant reads
$+0.56$ [$0.14$, $0.70$] but selects on agreement with the 1B target --- circular, a bound on
the aggregation effect, not evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:t3}). The inversion is
readout-specific: on DataDecide's 11-domain log-perplexity readout (3 seeds) there is none --- the 4M
ranking correlates with 1B at $r = +0.87$ and 4M pairwise accuracy is $85\%$
(Appendix~\ref{app:t3}); the failure mode lives on the accuracy readout, whose small-scale
tasks sit near chance.

\paragraph{Estimand, dependence, verdict.}
\label{sec:t3-estimand}
This SNR audits one published decision procedure on its own candidate set; it is not a
transferable constant of small-scale
pretraining. The 300 pairs share 25 recipes, so pair counts are descriptive coverage, with
inferential weight carried by the per-scale aggregates. \textbf{Verdict: accurate at its recommended scale, with a mapped operating
envelope}: the published claim reproduces at 150M; the SNR gradient delimits where the
instrument can see, and the 4M inversion marks where aggregation backfires.
